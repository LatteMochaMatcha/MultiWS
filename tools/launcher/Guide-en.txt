OC Base Mesh Workshop v2 — a base mesh generator for multi-limbed characters
============================================================================


[What this is]

A small tool for making 3D models of original characters (OCs).
Write one sentence describing the body, for example "two heads, two pairs of arms, nine tails, fox ears",
and it generates a matching 3D human body model.
After exporting, you can keep sculpting it in Blender, rig it with bones, or pose it. You can also take
screenshots and hand them to an image-generation AI as a reference for the body structure.

Parts it can make: 1-3 upper bodies (branching off from the waist, each with its own heads, arms and breasts),
1-5 heads, 0-4 pairs of arms, 0-4 pairs of breasts,
1-12 legs (an odd number is fine; centaur or spider lower bodies, or the all-human-legs "humantaur"),
0-9 tails, 0-3 pairs of wings, horns, animal ears (cat, fox, rabbit and elf ears), and multiple eyes.

The first time you open it, a "Tour" asks for a language and then shows you several areas of the interface
one step at a time; in the steps on adjusting individual limbs and posing, you can try things out on the model.
Click "Skip" if you don't want it. To see it again later, click "Tour" under the title.

The body comes in three styles. Switch them under "Body style" in the "Body shape" section of the right-hand panel:

  Mannequin             A colorless, smooth human body: one closed mesh, ready to sculpt right away.
                        Every part can be added and the body shape can be adjusted freely.
                        This is the default style and the one with the most features.
  Anime body            A base mesh with the proportions of an anime girl: slender limbs, a head with a pointed chin,
                        hands with fingers, feet with toes. No clothes; hair can be worn separately.
  Ready-made            Ready-made characters with hair, clothes, a face and textures, ten in all. Click the portraits
                        in the row under "Body shape" to switch (the default is the one with silver hair,
                        odd-colored eyes and a cyber-style dress). Each one needs a second or two to load the first
                        time it is selected.
                        You can also click "Load your own VRM…" under that row of portraits to use a character
                        you made yourself in VRoid Studio (VRM 0.x and 1.0 both work). The file is stored only on
                        this device and is still there the next time you open it.
                        Extra heads and arms are made by copying her own head or arm and attaching it; the export
                        carries textures and bones.
                        Centaur and spider lower bodies are separately made single-color horse and spider bodies,
                        attached under her skirt.
                        Humantaur adds a second copy of her own waist, pelvis and legs (skirt and shoes included)
                        behind her.
                        With legs "Side by side", the middle section of her pelvis is copied as is and spread out,
                        and the middle legs are assembled; with "Tandem", her pelvis and legs (skirt and shoes
                        included) are copied several times and attached one behind the other under the same section
                        of waist; "Gather at pelvis" works the same way, except that each pelvis sits tight
                        against the next and the legs spread outward.
                        With an odd number of legs, the one in the middle is her two legs merged into one.
                        Her hair can be swapped and dyed.

All three styles can wear hair: see the "Hair" entry under "How to use it" below.


[How to open it]

You need: Windows 10 or 11, and any one of Edge / Chrome / Firefox (the Edge that comes with Windows is fine).
No installation needed. When it opens, it goes online to check for a new version; it works fine without internet too.

1. First extract the zip file (don't double-click inside the zip).
2. Double-click "OC白模工坊.exe".
   - The first time, a blue "Windows protected your PC" screen may appear: click "More info", then "Run anyway".
     This is because the program isn't digitally signed, so Windows doesn't recognize it; every small tool made
     by an individual behaves this way.
   - If your antivirus software asks whether to allow it, choose allow. It only starts a local web page on your
     own computer. It goes online only to check for and download workshop updates, sends nothing about you,
     and keeps downloaded update files in %LOCALAPPDATA%\OC白模工坊.
3. A small black window appears, and at the same time your browser opens the workshop page automatically.
   If the browser doesn't open, copy the address in the black window (http://localhost:5210/) into the browser.
4. Don't close the black window while you are using the workshop. When you're done, close it and the program exits.


[Using it on a phone]

This exe works on computers only. Android phones have a separate installer, "OC白模工坊.apk"
(it is not in this zip and has to be sent separately):

1. Send the apk file to the phone (QQ, WeChat, a USB cable, anything works) and open it on the phone.
2. The phone will stop you with a warning about apps from unknown sources: choose allow, continue installing,
   or install anyway (each phone maker words it differently).
3. After it is installed there is a cyan "OC" icon on the home screen. Tap it to open the workshop. When it opens it
   goes online to check for a new version; it works fine without internet too.

It needs Android 10 or newer; iPhones and HarmonyOS NEXT phones can't install it.
Hold the phone upright: the model is in the top half of the screen and the settings are in the bottom half, and the
model stays visible as you scroll down; "Enlarge" at the top right makes the model fill the whole screen.
Drag with one finger to rotate, pinch with two fingers to zoom, drag with two fingers to pan, and tap a part to
select it.
Exported models and configs are in the "OC白模工坊" folder inside the phone's Downloads folder, and screenshots are
in the "OC白模工坊" album in the gallery.


[How to use it]

On the left is the 3D view: left-drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan. At the bottom left you can
switch Front / Side / Back / 3/4, Undo / Redo, and show the bones.
On the right is the settings panel, from top to bottom:

  Body description   Write the body in the box; the model updates by itself after you stop typing. Examples:
                     "two heads", "three pairs of arms", "nine tails", "four horns", "no tail",
                     "Asura", "centaur", "spider girl", "cat ears",
                     "165 cm", "male", "T-pose".
                     The Chinese phrasings work too, for example 「两个头」「三对手臂」「九条尾巴」「每个头四只角」
                     「没有尾巴」「三头六臂」「三面六臂」「九尾」「六翼」「阿修罗」「半人马」「蜘蛛女」「猫耳」
                     「身高 165」「八头身」「男性」「T-pose」.
                     "three heads" gives three heads huddled in a cluster; "three faces", 「三面六臂」 and "Asura"
                     give one head with three faces.
                     "humantaur" (人腿人马): like a centaur, but behind the body there isn't a horse body, there is a
                     section of human waist followed by another human pelvis and legs.
                     Hairstyles such as "twin tails", "long hair" and "bob cut" are recognized too; hair colors
                     such as "blonde hair" or "black hair" can only be used with Ready-made characters.
                     "two torsos", "three torsos" or "Geryon": one pelvis and set of legs carries two or three
                     upper bodies standing side by side and fanning out to both sides; each upper body gets
                     the full set of heads, arms and breasts. The two middle arms are changed to reach forward
                     (hanging down they would poke into the neighboring body).
                     The pelvis is 10% wider than one person's, so that the upper bodies can stand side by side on
                     it as in the drawings; the sides of the waist run smoothly, without a ring of bulges.
                     With the Anime body, the two upper bodies separate right from the pelvis: each has its own
                     waist and belly, and the V between them opens all the way to the top of the pelvis.
                     With a Ready-made character, everything above her waist (hair and clothes included) is copied
                     once and bent apart at the waist.
  Presets            12 ready-made combinations; click one to switch to it.
  Part counts        Adjust them one by one with the plus and minus buttons.
  Layout             "Multiple heads": "Clustered", "1 head, N faces" (one head with several faces) or "Wide torso";
                     "Multiple pairs of arms": "Stacked along the torso" or "Gathered at shoulders" (fanned out from
                     the shoulders);
                     "Three or more legs": "Auto", "Centaur", "Arachne", "Humantaur", "Tandem" (human legs front to
                     back), "Side by side" (in a row, sideways),
                     "Gather at pelvis" (the pelvis is still as wide as one person's, several pairs of legs are
                     packed under it and spread out below like a fan),
                     or "Pelvis row" (several complete pelvises in a row, two legs each; writing
                     "side by side hips" or 「横排多胯」 in the description works too).
                     With the Anime body, two heads set to "Wide torso" and a "Pelvis row" of two pelvises (4 legs),
                     the two people are merged from shoulder to hip into one wide torso:
                     two necks, a row of four breasts, the arms growing on the outer sides, and below that it
                     splits into two pelvises.
                     With an odd number of human legs, the extra one grows on the center line. For "Humantaur" and
                     "Tandem" you can also choose how many legs each pelvis carries, with
                     "Legs per pelvis (Humantaur, Tandem)": 6 legs with 3 per pelvis is "three front, three back"
                     (writing "6 legs, humantaur, 3 legs per pelvis" or 「前三后三」 in the description works too).
  Hair               Hairstyle: long straight, side ponytail, bob, twin tails, all taken from ready-made characters.
                     All three body styles can wear them.
                     The Mannequin and the Anime body are bald by default; once hair is put on, it is a
                     colorless base-mesh hair, joined with the head into one piece.
                     Ready-made characters wear their original hair by default; after changing it, the whole thing
                     is replaced by the new hair, with its colors and textures.
                     Hair color (Ready-made characters only): click a color to dye it, use "Custom" to pick your own,
                     and "Original color" to go back. Hairpins and ribbons are not dyed.
                     With several heads, click a head under "Dye which head" below (labeled L, C, R…; with
                     several upper bodies, "L body", "Mid body", "R body") and then choose a color, and only that
                     head is dyed; "All" dyes them together.
                     "Length" controls the part hanging below the face; "Volume" scales the whole hairstyle up or
                     down a little.
                     The first time you pick a certain hairstyle you wait a second or two (the data is being fetched).
  Body shape         Body style, gender, pose (A-pose / T-pose), and sliders for height, heads tall, bust
                     size, shoulder width, waist width, hip width, limb thickness, arm length, leg length and so on.
                     Pushed to either end they give very exaggerated cartoon proportions (limbs up to 3 times
                     thicker or down to 0.35 times).
                     Leg length doesn't change the height: when the legs are lengthened, the whole figure is scaled
                     down proportionally so it is still as tall as set.
                     On the Anime body, once the bust size is raised past 1.1 it gradually changes to a
                     large-breast shape (taken from a ready-made CC0 base body: two round breasts, slightly
                     drooping, with a fold below them), fully changed at 1.6.
                     Ready-made characters can be adjusted in everything except "Build"; these sliders stretch the
                     body together with its clothes, and the patterns on the clothes distort if you push them far.
  Manual tweaks      Click a part on the model (a particular arm, head or tail) and you can move, rotate and scale
                     it on its own (move up to 3 m, rotate ±180°, size 0.05-10 times).
                     With many legs, moving an outer leg makes the pelvis widen or narrow along with it.
                     Ready-made characters can be tweaked too: the parts follow the bones, and where they join the
                     body (shoulder, hip joint, neck) they stretch along; copied arms, tails, wings and horns move
                     as a whole piece; when a leg is moved, the piece of pelvis near that leg moves with it.
                     Its tweaks are recorded separately from those of the Mannequin and the Anime body.
  Mesh               "Fused (one piece)" (the default), or every part separate ("Separate parts (v1)").
  Config             Save all the settings into a file, or read them back.

All the settings are saved automatically in the browser and will still be there next time.


[Posing]

Once the body is set, click "Pose" at the top left of the 3D view and a set of handles lights up on the joints.
Click "Shape" to go back and keep editing the body.

  Large cyan circle    Hands, feet, pelvis. Drag a hand or foot and the elbow or knee bends by itself; if it can't
                       reach, the body follows. Drag the pelvis and the whole figure moves with the feet pinned
                       to the ground, and the legs bend by themselves.
  Yellow diamond       Elbows, knees. Dragging one makes the elbow (knee) point to another side while the hand
                       or foot stays put.
  Small white circle   Waist, chest, neck, head, top of the head, shoulders, hip joints, toe tips. Dragging the top
                       of the head tilts the head, dragging the chest bends over, dragging a shoulder shrugs
                       (drag further and the upper body leans with it).
  Click a handle       A colored rotation ring appears, which turns the segment from that joint outward (click
                       the wrist to turn the hand, click the shoulder to turn the whole arm).
                       Click empty space to put it away. Dragging empty space still turns the view.

  Small circle at an end   Tail tips, wing tips; on Ready-made characters, hair tips, skirt hems and sleeve cuffs.
                           Drag it and the whole chain bends along; a click turns the whole chain.
                           (The hair and hem handles are folded away normally; turn them on under
                           "Show which handles" in the "Pose" section.)

Extra arms, legs and heads each have their own handles. In the "Pose" section on the right you can: fold away the
groups of handles you don't need; choose whether the feet and hands stay in place when you drag elsewhere;
mirror left to right or flip horizontally; drop to the ground; reset; save a pose as a separate file and read it
back later, or apply it to another character. Undo / Redo work as usual.

  Hand gestures         The "Hand gestures" section takes care of the fingers. First choose which hand to pose (both
                        hands / left hand / right hand; with many arms you can also choose which pair), then click a
                        ready-made gesture: Relaxed, Spread, Together, Fist, Grip, Point,
                        Peace, 3, 4, Thumbs up, OK, Rock, 6, Gun, Claw, Pinch.
                        The six sliders below let you set how much each finger bends and spreads, one finger at a time.
                        To drag the fingertips directly: turn on "Fingers" under "Show which handles" in the "Pose"
                        section, zoom in on the hand and drag.
                        The Mannequin has two kinds of hand ("Shape → Layout → Hands"): the default "Separate fingers"
                        has every finger apart and can make any gesture; with "Joined fingers (mitten)" the four
                        fingers form one sheet, and only gestures like "Fist" and "Spread" look right.

  Expression & gaze     Ready-made characters only. In the "Expression & gaze" section on the right: click an
                        expression (Neutral, Happy, Smug, Angry, Sad, Surprised, > <), then use the sliders to set the
                        strength, close one eye on its own (left and right are her own left and right), open the
                        mouth, show fangs.
                        The two "Gaze" sliders turn the eyeballs; "Look at camera" makes her look toward the angle you
                        are looking at her from, and you click it again after turning the view.
                        With several heads, an extra row "Which face" appears above: one head can laugh while another
                        is angry.
                        Screenshots and exports taken in "Pose" show the face with the expression you set; switching
                        back to "Shape" shows the face with no expression.

  Pose library          60 poses: the first 8 are soft standing poses (standing naturally, hands on hips, waving,
                        peace sign, chin on hand…), and the rest are standing, walking, running, jumping, crouching,
                        sitting, lying down, punching, aiming a gun, swinging a sword… Click one to apply it; the figure
                        drops to the ground automatically and the fingers are posed too (gripping, open, relaxed).
                        If you aren't happy with it, keep dragging handles to adjust.
                        "Random pose" picks one at random. Centaurs and arachnes only take the pose from the waist up.
                        When a pose is applied and the arms poke into the body (with wide hips or big breasts), they
                        are pushed out a little; extra arms do the same movement as the arm they came from, and the ones
                        that can't do it (they would hit the body or other arms) stay as they were.
  Camera & lighting     Which side to look from; the larger the "FOV", the more exaggerated near-big-far-small
                        gets; which side the light shines from and how high; whether the ground shadow is shown.
                        It only affects how things look on screen and in screenshots, not the exported model.

After you have posed, changing between A-pose and T-pose, changing the body style, or adding or removing parts does
not change the pose along with it.
Exports and screenshots taken in "Pose" show the posed figure; exports taken in "Shape" are the rest pose.


[Export]

The export buttons are at the bottom of the panel. After you click one, the file is in your browser's download folder.

  Export base mesh (GLB)   The mesh. For sculpting; the most common choice.
  Rigged GLB               Mesh + skeleton + weights (finger bones included). Use it when you want to pose or animate
                           right away.
  OBJ                      The mesh, with quad faces kept.
  STL                      For 3D printing (scale it up 1000 times in the slicer, because the model is made in meters).
  PNG                      An image of the current view, with a transparent background.

GLB files exported from a Ready-made character carry textures; OBJ and STL have the shape only.


[Putting it into Blender]

1. Menu "File → Import → glTF 2.0 (.glb/.gltf)", and select the exported .glb file.
2. The model stands at the world origin, and the unit is meters. Press Numpad 1 to switch to the front view, and the
   character faces you.
3. Base mesh: select it and switch the mode at the top left to Sculpt Mode, and you can sculpt right away.
   Ready-made character: switch the viewport to Material Preview (the third of the row of spheres at the top right of
   the viewport) to see the textures.
4. Files with bones: click the armature, press Ctrl + Tab to enter Pose Mode, click a bone and press R to rotate it.
   The bone names are the common set (Hips, Spine, UpperArm_L, Head …), and extra parts carry a number
   (UpperArm_L.002, Head.002).


[Things to know]

- The face of the base mesh is very simple: just eye sockets, a nose and a chin, and no mouth. It is a starting point
  for sculpting, not a finished piece.
- What you see on screen is a fairly coarse preview, and a fine mesh is computed separately when you export. Hair on a
  base mesh looks thick and blurry in the preview and only becomes clear after export.
- The automatically computed weights are good enough for checking poses; real animation needs touching up.
- Posing bends the mesh directly by the weights: places that bend hard (the knees of a deep squat, a shoulder raised
  above the head) collapse a little. There are no joint limits, and nothing stops clipping.
- When "Mesh" is set to "Separate parts (v1)", every part is a separate piece and the joints overlap (like a ball-jointed
  doll): when an arm is raised, the ball at the shoulder shows. For a continuous skin, use "Fused (one piece)" in "Mesh".
- When the arms of a base mesh hang down, the shoulders and armpits look slightly different in "Pose" than in "Shape"
  (the arms are first spread out horizontally and then merged into one piece, so that nothing sticks together
  as a sheet of skin when they are raised).
- Posing a base mesh: while you drag a handle you see a preview that follows your hand (shoulders bulge, skin is
  stretched between the arms); under a second after you let go, the body is fused again to match the pose and swapped in.
  That is the final look, and it is also what GLB / OBJ / STL exports of a posed figure contain. With many parts, or on
  a phone, the swap is slower.
  If you move the pose again while it is still computing, the old one stops immediately and the new one doesn't queue up.
- When a finer mesh is computed (after you stop, when you let go while posing, on export), several CPU cores are used
  together, so the CPU suddenly maxes out and then goes idle when it is done.
  If a game or another program that eats a lot of CPU is running at the same time, both get slower.
- The Anime body is only re-fused when its arms are set to "Gathered at shoulders" (with one arm on each
  shoulder, the original shoulder looks more natural and is kept).
  Its legs are moved over, after fusing, in the old way, the same as what you see while dragging.
  In poses where the legs move a lot (crouching, sitting, lunging, chopping), the arm next to a leg gets a few
  stretched faces pulled out of it; this isn't fixed yet.
- Poses from the pose library applied to arms set to "Gathered at shoulders": if the pose is close to hanging (standing, walking,
  sitting and the like), the arms are posed together and are still fanned out; if it's very different (arms folded,
  phoning and the like), only the arm whose direction is closest is posed and the others stay put. It used to pose only
  the first one, which then overlapped the one that was still hanging.
- After posing, a wrist that pokes into a thigh or another arm, or two wrists next to each other, usually still gets its
  hand sewn on; the side that is squeezed keeps a shallow groove hugging the wrist.
  A few still can't be sewn (more often on the Anime body, when the wrist pokes into a leg that hasn't been moved
  away yet): that hand seals itself and overlaps in its old place, the mesh is then more than one piece, and the export
  tells you so; move the hand away a little and it's fine.
- When you stretch an arm longer or thicker by hand, the hand doesn't deform: it just follows the wrist; when stretched
  longer the hand stays the same, and when made thicker the hand grows along.
- If you want the joints of a rigged base mesh to look better once it is in Blender where they turn a lot: click the
  mesh → Modifiers (blue wrench) → Armature → tick "Preserve Volume".
- With arms set to "Gathered at shoulders", the several arms on the same shoulder share one lump of shoulder: raise just
  one, and a little piece of skin gets pulled up between it and the next. For fully independent arms, set "Mesh" to
  "Separate parts (v1)".
- The hands of the Mannequin and the Anime body are a ready-made hand (with each finger apart), sewn onto the body
  at the wrist into one piece; a very shallow seam can be seen around the wrist. If the Mannequin in a setting saved
  earlier is still on "Joined fingers (mitten)", click "Separate fingers" under "Shape → Layout → Hands".
- The pieces of a Ready-made character overlap each other without being fused, so the seams are fairly stiff; if manual
  tweaks move a part far away, the place that joins it to the body stretches.
  It can't do the number of eyes yet. With legs in tandem, the pelvises are placed overlapping each other and there is a
  crease in between; with three or more legs on one pelvis, the middle section of the pelvis is copied as is and spread
  out, so the pattern in the middle repeats a few times, and where each two copies join there is a soft fold.
  The first time you switch to a centaur or spider there is a half-second stutter while the beast body is computed.
- The shoulder width, waist width, hip width, limb thickness and build of the Anime body widen, narrow, thicken
  and thin it from her original shape; hip width only works on the one standing pair of human legs (it stays unchanged
  for centaur, arachne, a single leg, or three or more legs on one pelvis).
- The feet of the Anime body are, like the hands, a ready-made foot (taken from Kotori, with five toes), sewn into
  one piece above the ankle.
- While you drag a slider on screen, the mesh is a coarse one; a quarter of a second after you stop it is swapped for
  a fine one (as fine as the export), and small details such as the eyes and the mouth can only be seen clearly after it
  is swapped in.
- If all the body-shape sliders are pulled to the minimum at the same time and combined with an "Arachne" or "Gather at
  pelvis" lower body, one hand or foot of the Anime body can't be sewn on (the mesh has an extra piece, and the
  export tells you).


[FAQ]

The left side is empty and says the 3D view could not start
    WebGL is not enabled in the browser. Switch to a recent Edge or Chrome, or turn on "hardware acceleration" in the
    browser settings.

The black window says the port is in use
    Close the other program and try again. If a workshop is already running, double-clicking again just reopens the
    page; it won't start a second one.

I want to start over
    Click "Standard humanoid" in the presets. The body-shape sliders do not reset with it; drag them back yourself.

The page doesn't open any more; or, when switching to "Anime body" or "Ready-made", a message below says
loading failed… and that it can't reach the workshop program
    The black window has been closed. The page needs it to work: a page that is already open can still play with the
    Mannequin, but the data for the other two styles can't be fetched.
    Just double-click the exe again. Go back to the original page and it will retry by itself.
